# Final Presentations and Reports

Your group will present your course project to the class and submit a written report. Both will be assessed with the fixed criteria and the [rubric your group designed]({{ '/rubric-design/' | relative_url }}). Though your report will only be graded by the instructors, your "intended" audience for both is the whole class: students and instructors from different fields should be able to follow your project's motivations, methodology, contributions, discussion, and implications.

## Final presentation

Your presentation should cover:

1. **Your problem statement:** what you set out to do, why someone in the relevant field would care, and why people outside the relevant field should also care. Assume your audience does not know your field's norms!
2. **Who your group is:** (Briefly) Skillsets and disciplinary backgrounds, short description of your collaboration
3. **What you did:** experimental setting, assumptions, data, methods, evaluations, other contributions. Explain rationale behind choices, especially those that may be non-obvious to someone from another discipline
4. **What you observed:** your progress and main results (so far), + at least one concrete example (can be: an input and output, an annotated item, an error case, a demo (or a recording of one))
5. **Challenges and roadblocks:** something that changed since your proposal, limitations, challenges, lessons learned
6. **Future work:** anything you will finish between the presentation and the report, AND work that would be cool for someone (maybe even you) to do after the semester

Expectations:

- Every group member should speak, especially about their own contributions
- Presentations should report "substantial" progress (somewhere in between "first sign of life" and "final" results, ideally more than "preliminary") which may or may not include progress made via other course assignments, but feel free to also describe "substantial" future work outside the scope of this semester
- Define or drop field-specific jargon, from *both* sides of your group!
- You will be assessed on the presentation that you are able to give in the allotted time
- Ask questions! (Similar format as for intro presentations-- form to come)

## Final report

Required sections (from the [rubric design activity]({{ '/rubric-design/' | relative_url }})):

1. **Abstract.** You may build on an abstract from the [cross-disciplinary writing exercise]({{ '/cross-disciplinary-writing/' | relative_url }}).
2. **Introduction:** a clearly defined, well-motivated problem and scope.
3. **Related work:** make sure each of the fields related to your project is represented! Related work should describe the general landscape as well as the specific gap in literature that your work is addressing
4. **Methodology:** data (where it came from, how much, any annotation), models and tools, evaluation
5. **Experiments and results:** if your primary contribution is an artifact or interface, experiments and analyses may be secondary to a thorough description of what you built, but this section should in any case contain evidence that your contributions do what you say they do
6. **Discussion and limitations:** what your results do and do not show, and what you would do next.

Also required, not counted toward the page limit:

- **Contributions statement:** what each member did. Beyond the most "direct" contributions named in the proposal, make sure to also describe administrative and other work that facilitated communication, collaboration, and project organization.
- **AI use statement:** see [AI use](#ai-use) below.
- **Code and data:** a link to your repository (or a zip), with a README that says how to reproduce your main results. If data cannot be shared, say why and describe it instead.
- **Rubric self-assessment (appendix):** one row for each criterion in your group's approved rubric, including each member's individual criteria. For each, give the marks you think you earned (noting if you took the fallback path or met a stretch goal), a sentence of justification, and where the evidence is (report section, table, repo folder). Each member fills in the rows for their own individual criteria.
- **Other details:** hyperparameters, system prompts, screenshots of interfaces, etc. that are not in the main body

Notes:

- Negative results or results that come with limitations are fine! As in your rubrics, you are assessed on the work you did and how clearly you reason about it, not on outcomes you could not have predicted.
- If your project changed since your proposal or rubric, say what changed and why. If a rubric criterion no longer applies, talk to us *before* the report is due.
- You may reuse text and results from your proposal, labs, and exercises.

## AI use

**For the project work itself,** AI use is allowed for things like writing code, exploring data, generating or labeling examples, searching for related work, as long as you document it. No need to e.g. submit full chat history, but things like system prompts used to generate synthetic data are part of good practice for reproducible science! You are responsible for everything you submit, including the correctness of AI-assisted code and the existence and content of every paper you cite.

**For the report and slides,** all ideas, claims, structure, and text should be your own. AI should not draft your report. You may use AI for assistance with narrower, *local* changes: initial figures or figure revisions, checking grammar and spelling, suggesting revisions to text you have already written, and slide layout and visuals. Be careful with this, and consider using a dedicated grammar checker over a general-purpose LLM: many general-purpose LLMs tend to make changes beyond what you explicitly ask for, stripping out your voice and undermining intentional decisions you may have made to communicate specific ideas. Your report and presentation should read to us (the instructors) as written by (human) members of your group. 

Your **AI use statement** (required, but not counted toward the page limit) should cover:

- which tools and models you used, and for which parts of the project, report, and slides
- how you checked the outputs you kept
- what the benefits and trade-offs turned out to be, compared with what you expected in your rubric justification
- anything you tried to use AI for and abandoned, and why

## Optional individual comments

If there is anything you would like the instructors to know separately from your group (e.g. about how your group worked together, as feedback for the instructors about the assignment's structure, as additional context for your own contributions, etc.), you may submit it through a separate, optional Canvas assignment (TBA). You do not need to submit anything there.

## How you will be graded

- **Fixed criteria (35%):** clear, organized communication in the final report and presentation; a clearly defined, well-motivated problem and scope; situation in related work; documentation and reproducibility of implementations.
- **Your group's rubric (65%):** your group and individual criteria, applied by the instructors with your group's self-assessment as input. Stretch goals earn up to 2 bonus points across the whole project.
