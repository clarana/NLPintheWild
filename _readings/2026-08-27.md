Works referenced in lecture 2, grouped by the perspective on history that each one
illustrates. Entries without a link were named in lecture without a specific citation.

## Main reading

- [To Build Our Future, We Must Know Our Past: Contextualizing Paradigm Shifts in Natural Language Processing](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.822/) (Gururaja, Bertsch, Na, Widder, Strubell; EMNLP 2023). [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.822.pdf)
  A timeline synthesized from 26 interviews with professors and industry researchers, plus quantified data over ACL Anthology papers.

## A philosophical roots perspective

- Turing, A. M. "[Computing Machinery and Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433)." *Mind* LIX(236):433-460, 1950. The Turing test.
- Shannon information theory work, 1950s. n-gram source models and perplexity-style evaluation.
- Zipf's Law, 1932.
- "Universal language" as described by Leibniz and Descartes in the 1600s: *De Arte Combinatoria*, and the Alphabet of Human Thought.

## A key people perspective

Three groups worth tracking: people doing pivotal research (individual researchers and
PIs), people with power over research agendas (department heads, funding agency leaders),
and people who collaborated (in academic departments, for grants, at industry labs).

- [Walker, D. E. *Artificial Intelligence and Language Processing: A Directory of Research Personnel*](https://aclanthology.org/J76-1006.pdf). *American Journal of Computational Linguistics*, February 1976.

## A hardware perspective

- Mark I Perceptron, 1958. An early visual classifier that learned parameters from data.
- Minsky, M. and Papert, S. *Perceptrons*. MIT Press, 1969.
- Raina, R., Madhavan, A., Ng, A. Y. "[Large-scale Deep Unsupervised Learning using Graphics Processors](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ang/papers/icml09-LargeScaleUnsupervisedDeepLearningGPU.pdf)." *ICML* 2009.

## A funding and incentives perspective

- The 1954 Georgetown-IBM demo, and "machine translation just around the corner" -- repeatedly.
- ALPAC. *Language and machines: computers in translation and linguistics*, 1966. John Pierce was hired as ALPAC chair. The report ended most US machine translation funding.
- Pierce, J. R. "Whither speech recognition?" 1969.
- The neural AI winter, after Minsky and Papert.
- Charles Wayne, 1986, "Glamour and Deceit."

## A seminal (empirical) works perspective

- Marcus, M., Santorini, B., Marcinkiewicz, M. A. "[Building a Large Annotated Corpus of English: The Penn Treebank](https://aclanthology.org/J93-2004/)." *Computational Linguistics* 19(2):313-330, 1993. A shared corpus, widely used and cited for decades.
- Krizhevsky, A., Sutskever, I., Hinton, G. "[ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Abstract.html)." *NIPS* 2012. AlexNet: a big deep learning success.
- Mikolov, T., Chen, K., Corrado, G., Dean, J. "[Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3781)." 2013. word2vec.
- Pennington, J., Socher, R., Manning, C. "[GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation](https://aclanthology.org/D14-1162/)." *EMNLP* 2014. Trained vector word embeddings.
- Vaswani, A. et al. "[Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)." *NIPS* 2017. The Transformer architecture.
- Devlin, J., Chang, M.-W., Lee, K., Toutanova, K. "[BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1423/)." *NAACL* 2019. With GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019), establishes "pre-train then fine-tune."
- Brown, T. et al. "[Language Models are Few-Shot Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)." 2020. GPT-3, and in-context learning.
- ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022). Optimization for dialogue.

## An NLP as a special case of ML perspective

No new works. This perspective re-reads three items above: the 1966 ALPAC/Pierce funding
dry spell (and the argument that it was not the main cause of the AI winter), Charles
Wayne's DARPA program and its held-out sets, and "Attention Is All You Need" as
everything-as-sequence-learning.

- Smith et al., 2021.
- The slide title refers to Rich Sutton's 2019 essay "The Bitter Lesson," which argues that general methods plus compute beat human-encoded knowledge.
