Reading Responses

For each class session that has assigned readings, you must submit a short reading response by 9:00 AM ET on the day of class. Responses are submitted via Gradescope.

Your response should be roughly 200–300 words and address:

  1. A brief (1–2 sentence) summary of the main contribution of each paper.
  2. One strength of the work.
  3. One limitation, open question, or critique.
  4. One question you’d like to discuss in class.

Responses are graded on a ✓+/✓/✓− scale. You may skip up to 3 responses without penalty.

Paper Presentation

Each student will give one 20-minute presentation (15 min talk + 5 min Q&A) on a paper from the course schedule. Presentation slots will be sign-up starting January 21. Sign up via the Google Sheet linked on Piazza.

Presentation Guidelines

  • Cover the paper’s motivation, methods, experiments, and conclusions clearly.
  • Contextualize the work relative to other readings and course themes.
  • Discuss limitations and possible future directions.
  • Prepare 2–3 discussion questions for the class.

Research Project

The research project is the central component of this course. Projects may be done individually or in groups of 2–3 students. The goal is to produce work of publishable quality — either a novel empirical study, a system paper, a theoretical contribution, or a thorough literature survey combined with new analysis.

Proposal (Due: Feb 6)

Submit a 1–2 page proposal (excluding references) describing:

  1. The research question or problem you are addressing.
  2. Why the problem is interesting and relevant to the course themes.
  3. Your proposed approach / methodology.
  4. Relevant related work (5–10 references).
  5. A timeline with milestones.
  6. Team members (if applicable) and each member’s role.

Progress Report (Due: Mar 13)

Submit a 2–3 page progress report describing:

  1. What you have accomplished so far.
  2. Any changes to your original plan and why.
  3. Preliminary results (even if negative).
  4. Remaining work and updated timeline.
  5. Challenges or blockers, and how you plan to address them.

Final Presentation (Apr 8–15)

Each team will give a 10-minute talk (7 min + 3 min Q&A) during class in the last two weeks of the semester. Presentations will be scheduled in advance.

Final Paper (Due: Apr 25)

Submit a paper formatted in ACL format. Suggested length: 8 pages of content + unlimited references. The paper should be self-contained and describe your problem, methods, experiments, results, and conclusions.
